## Maintaining this plan

This section owns how TODO is updated. General working principles belong in AGENTS; the review skill owns the review procedure. Keep this file focused on work that remains and dependencies on accepted decisions.

1. Give each task a stable ID, severity or priority, evidence status, relevant paths, expected outcome, validation criteria, and dependencies. Keep hypotheses distinguishable from validated defects; preserve reviewer classifications and record user dispositions. Do not recycle IDs.
2. Keep unassigned work compact. Before implementation, establish the assigned task's full boundary: intent, dependencies, implementation direction, affected compatibility surface, non-goals, validation, and done criteria. The compatibility surface names every HTTP contract, configuration setting or on-disk layout whose signature or semantics the change could affect, and states which are preserved. Python modules are internal unless DESIGN declares a public API. The boundary lives in this file, because the review working record is machine-local; the working record links to it rather than copying it, and the reviewer is given its location. State shared group constraints once; retain distinct acceptance criteria for each task.
3. Record accepted decisions with their rationale and affected tasks in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). Keep unresolved choices explicit. When a decision changes, record its supersession and update affected boundaries and dependencies before the implementation commit.
4. Use explicit dependencies and readiness to guide execution. Do not build phase-closure fences or imply that every non-blocking issue must land before unrelated work can proceed.
5. When closing a task, create `completed_work/<ID>.md` in the same commit that removes its full active entry and execution boundary. Preserve its origin, accepted boundary and superseding decisions, revalidation, implementation or other disposition, validation commands and results with revision/environment provenance, required or performed mutation evidence, review findings and their validation/dispositions, limitations, and cleanup. State when checks or review were not performed and why; an explicit review waiver is not a passed review. Retain essential evidence in the record or durable linked artifacts under the [review skill's evidence-retention rules](.agents/skills/adversarial-review/SKILL.md#evidence-retention); temporary paths alone are not a completion archive. The committed archive is the only evidence other machines see, so it MUST be self-contained: a record MAY link other committed files, but never machine-local paths such as session scratch or a working record. Omit inapplicable sections. Each task keeps its own record and acceptance evidence. Link shared evidence by section instead of repeating it, identifying the applicable candidate and any differences; separately committed tasks retain their own validation, mutation outcomes and review verdict. Sharing a record section does not establish eligibility to reuse validation. Leave one source line summarizing the outcome and linking the record under dispositions. Update references, dependencies, and affected counts or indexes; current operations belong in README. Use stable headings for linked sections; short fields may remain inline.
6. Record deferral or acceptance without change explicitly, with the reason and any revisit trigger. Closing an obsolete task or accepting it without change requires the completion record and disposition link above; deferred work remains active. Do not silently drop inherited work or treat a documentation rewrite as an implementation fix.
7. Keep evidence tied to its revision and provenance. Remove obsolete status inventories; historical review results do not certify an executor's current gates. Check local links and ID/dependency consistency after restructuring this file.

### T16: Caption language matching

- **Intent:** DESIGN 5.6 with C12: well-formed language candidates, and the selection of at most one caption track.
- **Implementation direction:** `is_well_formed(tag)` matches RFC 5646 section 2.1's `Language-Tag` ABNF case-insensitively, including private-use and grandfathered tags, without registry checks: well-formed, not valid, as DESIGN requires. `select_track(candidates, tracks)` takes ordered candidates and the listed tracks, each exposing `language_code` and `is_generated` (the attribute names of `youtube-transcript-api`'s `Transcript`, described by a `Protocol`), and returns the chosen track or `None`. A candidate matches a code when its subtags, compared case-insensitively, are a prefix of the code's subtags. Candidates are tried in order against manual tracks, then in order against generated ones; within one candidate and kind an exact match wins, then listed order. The selector chooses only among the tracks it is given; T20 passes listed tracks and never translations, which satisfies rule 5.
- **Compatibility surface:** the later `languages` request field and the recorded track code and kind (T19, T24).
- **Non-goals:** normalizing codes (such as `iw` to `he`), BCP/ISO conversion and the source revision's whitelist, which stay in `youtube-whisperer`.
- **Validation:** tests for the ABNF's productions and rejections, every example in DESIGN 5.6 rule 1, no shorter-tag fallback, manual-first across the whole list, exact-before-listed order, and empty results. Mutation evidence for manual-first ordering and for the subtag boundary.

### T17: Staging layout

- **Intent:** DESIGN 5.4 and 5.13's marker, with C23 and C33: the filesystem helpers that the worker and reconciliation share.
- **Implementation direction:** in `youtube_collector/staging.py`:
  - `marker_present(root)` is true only when `.youtube-collector-root` exists at the root; any error reading the root counts as absent.
  - `media_name`, `captions_name` and `playlist_dir_name` build `Title [id].mp4`, `Title [id].srt` and `Title [id]` with pathlib-extensions' `build_filename(…, max_length=220)`, with OS-reserved characters replaced in the ID tag too; `ValueError` when nothing of the title fits.
  - `find_artifacts(directory, video_id, suffix)` lists the regular files, following symlinks, whose names end literally with ` [<id>]<suffix>`, sorted; a missing directory yields none and other errors propagate.
  - `captions_name_for(title, video_id, existing_media)` gives the stem of the single existing MP4 plus `.srt`, or `captions_name` otherwise (C33).
  - `publish(target, write)` stages through `StagedFile` under `NEVER`, which creates a missing target directory, and returns the published path, or `None` when a competing file appeared at the target meanwhile; `write` receives the stage path.
  - `file_present(path)` reports whether a regular file exists and propagates errors other than its absence, so a caller never mistakes an unreadable share for a removed file.
  - `relative_to_root(root, path)` gives the POSIX path that responses carry (DESIGN section 10).
  - `pyproject.toml` gains the mypy `ignore_missing_imports` override for `pathlib_extensions`, as its comment prescribes.
- **Compatibility surface:** the on-disk layout of DESIGN 5.4 (names, playlist folders, `.staging-*` stage directories, the marker name) and the staging-relative paths that T24 will return.
- **Non-goals:** deciding outcomes or calling yt-dlp (T20, T21), scavenging stage directories (DESIGN 5.4), and creating the marker (DESIGN 5.13).
- **Validation:** tests for the 220-byte limit with multi-byte titles, reserved characters, titles that cannot fit, ID matching treated literally (glob characters, IDs that are suffixes of others, other suffixes, directories), the C33 stem rule for zero, one and two MP4s, publication including a competing file and a failing writer leaving no stage directory, `file_present` raising on errors, and the marker. Mutation evidence for the literal-ID match and for `publish` never overwriting, which protect against data loss.

### T18: Failure categories

- **Intent:** DESIGN 5.9's category hint and raw message.
- **Implementation direction:** a `Category` string enum with the six DESIGN values; `categorize(error)` and `failure_message(error)` (the error's text, or its type name when the text is empty). Typed rules are applied first along the error's chain (`exc_info` as yt-dlp's `DownloadError` carries it, `__cause__` and `__context__`): `youtube-transcript-api`'s `RequestBlocked`, `PoTokenRequired`, `AgeRestricted` and `FailedToCreateConsentCookie` are `blocked`; `VideoUnavailable`, `VideoUnplayable` and `InvalidVideoId` are `unavailable`; `YouTubeDataUnparsable` is `extractor`; `YouTubeRequestFailed` and `requests` exceptions are `network`; `TimeoutError`, `ConnectionError` and `socket.gaierror` are `network`, and other `OSError`s `filesystem`. Otherwise case-insensitive message patterns are tried in the order `blocked`, `unavailable`, `filesystem`, `network`, `extractor`, falling back to `internal`. yt-dlp is not imported: its errors are recognized by attribute and message, so T18 does not depend on T20.
- **Compatibility surface:** the later `category` and `message` fields of failure records (T19, T24); categories are a documented hint, and the message stays authoritative.
- **Non-goals:** deciding which errors are failures (T21), and the phase, which the caller supplies.
- **Validation:** tests with representative yt-dlp messages for each category, every listed caption exception, chained and wrapped errors, and the fallback. No mutation evidence is required: categories are hints that never change an outcome.
